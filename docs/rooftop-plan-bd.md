## 1. Correction: "CDK worldwide" is two companies

Francisco Partners bought CDK's international business in 2020 for **$1.45bn** and rebranded it **Keyloop**. CDK is North America only. **Fortellis serves US and Canada only** — no international presence.

Keyloop runs **~16,000 dealer sites across 90+ countries** (EMEA + Asia), with its own Open Platform carrying Sales, Service and Customers APIs. Going global = two integrations, two certifications, two listings, sequenced.

Upside: this roughly **doubles the reachable market**, and the halves are comparable in size. Keyloop is UK-headquartered, you're UK-based, and the US service lane is defended by Xtime/UpdatePromise/Kimoby/Impel. **Europe via Keyloop may be the easier beachhead.**

| Platform | Timing | Reach |
|---|---|---|
| Fortellis Marketplace | Year 1 | CDK Drive, US + Canada |
| Keyloop Open Platform | Year 2 | ~16,000 sites, 90+ countries |
| Tekion / Dealertrack | Year 3+ | Only once the first two produce |

**Key numbers:** 16,990 US franchised dealerships (NADA 2025); ~6,800 CDK US rooftops at ~40% DMS share; $14,400 annual revenue per rooftop at $1,200/mo blended.

⚠️ **CDK figure caveat:** CDK claims "15,000+ NA rooftops" AND "~40% US DMS share." These can't both be true (16,990 US + ~3,500 Canada → 15,000 would be ~73%). This plan uses 40%. **Verify with your CDK rep — it's the single number that most moves the model.**

This is a ~$223M serviceable market, not a billion-dollar one. Excellent bootstrapped business; hard classic-venture story.

## 3. Pricing

Comparables: point tools $200–600/rooftop/mo; reporting/analytics $150–900; the DMS itself $2,000–10,000+. $600 is the ceiling for anything read as a point tool — the four-module ledger is what breaks it.

| Tier | Modules | List | Expected close |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business Office | AR & schedule chase | $499 | $450–499 |
| Fixed Ops | Declined work + parts arrival | $999 | $850–999 |
| Full Ledger | All four + group roll-up | $1,799 | $1,200–1,500 |

**Model on $1,200, quote $1,799.** Early vertical SaaS realises 60–75% of list.

### Group discount curve

| Rooftops | Discount | Effective | Example | Group ARR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | List | $1,799 | 3 | $64,800 |
| 5–9 | −10% | $1,619 | 7 | $136,000 |
| 10–24 | −20% | $1,439 | 15 | $259,000 |
| 25–49 | −30% | $1,259 | 30 | $453,000 |
| 50+ | −40% | $1,079 | 75 | $971,000 |

One 75-rooftop group ≈ **$1M ARR from a single relationship**. Ten group deals gets most of the way to year three. GTM = **~50 serious conversations** with group fixed-ops directors and CFOs, not a volume motion.

**Flat price, revenue-share reporting.** Never take a % of recovered dollars — attribution disputes will consume you.

## 8. Decision gates

1. **The number** (days 1–14) — Capitol recovery report. *Gate: >$500k declined over 24 months, or stop.*
2. **Product proof** (weeks 3–14) — obligation model, consent ledger, attribution; service + parts, internal queue only. *Gate: advisor opens queue unprompted 5 days/week for a month AND attribution shows recovered dollars.*
3. **Repeatability** (months 4–9) — Fortellis certified, listing live, first group outside Capitol. *Gate: two paying customers with no personal relationship to you. Otherwise you have a consultancy.*
4. **Second continent** (months 10–18) — Keyloop adapter, UK pilot. *Gate: adapter ships in under eight weeks, or the DMS abstraction failed.*
5. **Scale or sell** (months 19–36) — *Gate: at $5M+ ARR with attribution data and sticky group contracts, strategics (Cox, CDK, Solera, Keyloop) pay 6–10× → $35–80M.*

## The shape of this business

A ~$223M serviceable market across two marketplaces, priced at 5% of the labour it replaces, 2–6 month CAC payback, ~$250k peak funding need in year one only.

Not a venture-scale TAM and doesn't need to be. Reaches $5.5M ARR in three years on its own cash; worth $35–80M to a strategic buyer wanting integrated fixed-ops. The binding constraint isn't market size or capital — it's **12–18 months of undivided attention**, and whether attribution is good enough that a controller believes the number.

**Scores:** Quality 55 · Motivation 88 · Winnability 82 · Fit 70

**The number that matters:**

```
$195,115 revenue ÷ ~1,500 accounts = $130 per account per year
```

That is implausibly low. A single annual extinguisher certification for a small
commercial account generally exceeds it — before six-year maintenance, before
twelve-year hydrostatic testing, before any deficiency work. Three readings:

1. **A large share of the 1,500 are dormant.** The owners stopped calling years
   ago. This is *exactly* the lapsed-due-date thesis from `NICHE-THESIS.md` §3.1.
2. "1,500 accounts" is historical or inflated.
3. The accounts are genuinely tiny — single-extinguisher customers.

**This one listing is a live test of the entire niche thesis, and the test is
cheap.** One diligence request settles it: *export the account list with
last-service-date, next-due-date, device count, and last invoice amount.* If a
meaningful share of those 1,500 are real compliance accounts that simply stopped
being called, the thesis is real and this is a goldmine hiding behind a $93k SDE.
If they are 1,500 one-extinguisher accounts, the thesis needs rethinking — which
is worth knowing before deal 1 rather than after.

The customer mix is the right one: condos, hospitals, property management are
precisely the recurring-compliance buyers the thesis targets. The 47.8% SDE
margin is consistent with a semi-retired owner running lean and not chasing work
— which is the profile that leaves a base to recover.

**Fails the buy box on two counts:** SDE $93k is below the $150k floor, and
Jupiter (Palm Beach County) is ~3.5 hours from Tampa — a clear breach of the
45-minute rule.

**Recommendation: sign the NDA anyway.** Not to buy it today, but because the
account-list answer is the cheapest possible validation of the whole strategy.
If the answer is good, reconsider whether the metro should be Palm Beach /
Treasure Coast rather than Tampa.

be one of several, competing on cash, which is the game we specifically decided
not to play.

**Recommendation: one call, one question** — what percentage of revenue is
recurring inspection/service versus product sales? If recurring is over 60%, it
deserves a second look despite geography. Under 40%, pass immediately.

## 4. LICENSING — RESOLVED, AND BETTER THAN EXPECTED

The open question in `NICHE-THESIS.md` §6.2 was whether the licensee universe is
enumerable. **It is, and it is public.**

**Regulator:** Florida Department of Financial Services, Division of State Fire
Marshal, **Bureau of Fire Prevention** — Regulatory Licensing section.

**Statutes and rules (verify current text before relying on any of it):**

| Scope | Statute | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Fire equipment dealers / extinguisher service | § 633.304 F.S. | 69A-21 F.A.C. |
| Fire Protection Systems Contractor | § 633.318 F.S. | 69A-46 F.A.C. |

Ch. 633 defines graded contractor classes — **Contractor I and Contractor II**
among them, with scope limits tied to system size (a Contractor I/II may design
systems of 49 or fewer sprinklers). There is a separate **Water-Based Inspector
permit**. Oversight sits with the **Florida Fire Safety Board**.

**The pipeline tool — public licensee search:**

> **https://www.citizenserve.com/120** → Regulatory Licensing → **"Search for a license"**

This is the complete target universe, published by the state, free. Cross-referenced
against Sunbiz entity age, officer continuity, and UCC filings (per `SPEC.md`
§3.1), it produces a ranked map of every fire-services acquisition candidate in
any Florida metro — which is not a position available to us in any unlicensed
vertical, and is the strongest single argument for this niche.

The portal also exposes inspections, plans review, and boiler safety search —
worth examining later for adjacent signal.

**Still required before any offer** (unchanged, and this sweep does not shortcut
it): confirm with the Bureau which exact license classes each target holds,
whether they transfer on a stock versus asset purchase, and what a qualifier must
hold. One listing claims licensure "can be obtained during transition" — treat
that as a seller's claim, not guidance.

## 5. WHAT THIS SWEEP CHANGES

1. **Portals are for comps, not deals — now evidenced, not asserted.** Five
   searches across every major marketplace produced zero buy-box matches and two
   genuinely actionable leads, **both of which were FSBO posts with no broker.**
   That ratio is the argument for the whole sourcing strategy.

2. **Build the licensee-registry pipeline first,** ahead of any other software.
   It is public, it is enumerable, and it is the only channel that reaches the
   businesses we actually want.

3. **Distribution-versus-service is a new hard filter.** Candidate B looked good
   on financials and is probably the wrong business model entirely. A fire
   *products* business has no due-date engine and no deficiency reports — the two
   levers the AI thesis rests on. Add to the `SPEC.md` §4 kill gate:
   **recurring inspection/service revenue must exceed 50%.**

4. **Revenue-per-account is the single best diagnostic in this niche,** and it
   should be a computed field on every deal. Candidate A's $130/account is
   simultaneously the biggest red flag and the biggest opportunity in the sweep.
   No other metric surfaces a dormant base that fast.

5. **The exit multiple is the weakest link in the thesis.** Entry multiples are
   now evidenced at 2.75–3.4×. The 6–8× platform exit is not. Get verified
   transaction data before committing capital on the strength of multiple
   expansion.

6. **Geography may need to bend.** The two best-scoring opportunities are in
   Palm Beach County and Orlando. Neither is Tampa. If the pattern holds after
   the registry sweep, pick the metro where the deals actually are rather than
   defending the original choice.
